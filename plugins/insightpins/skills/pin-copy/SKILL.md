# Pinterest pin copy

Pinterest is a visual search engine: people search for ideas and plans, and Pinterest matches pins
by their text (title, description, alt text, board, the linked page and the words on the image).
Good copy is specific, keyword-led and honest.

## A pin has several different texts

| Text | Where it shows | Length | Job |
| - | - | - | - |
| **On-image headline** | On the pin image | 3-8 words ideal | Stop the scroll; state the benefit |
| **Subtitle** (optional) | On the image, under the headline | Up to ~12 words | Add the detail that makes it click-worthy |
| **Pin title** | Under or beside the pin | Up to 100 characters; front-load the first ~40 | Main keyword + benefit |
| **Pin description** | Pin page, and used for search | Up to 500 characters; first ~50 matter most | Keywords in natural sentences + soft CTA |
| **Alt text** | Screen readers, search | Up to 500 characters; 1-2 sentences | Literally describe the image |
| **Board** | Where the pin is saved | 2-5 words | Keyword-named topic, not "My Stuff" |

Write them separately. The headline is not the title, and the title is not the description.

## Keywords

1. **If an InsightPins keyword tool is connected**, use it first: look up the topic, pick one primary
   keyword with good volume and close relevance, and 2-4 related secondary keywords.
2. **Otherwise**, derive them from the page: its title, headings and the words a person would type
   into Pinterest search to find this ("easy chicken dinner recipes", "small bathroom storage ideas").
   Prefer specific 2-4 word phrases over single generic words. Don't present guessed keywords as search data.

Where keywords go: primary keyword at the start of the pin title, in the first sentence of the
description, in the board name and, naturally, in the alt text. Secondary keywords in the description.

## Headlines

Use the formulas in [references/title-formulas.md](references/title-formulas.md). Rules:
- Concrete benefit over cleverness: "15-Minute Pasta Dinners", not "Pasta Love".
- Numbers when the content is a list, using the real count.
- Name the audience or situation when it sharpens the promise ("for Small Kitchens", "on a Budget").
- Title Case or sentence case consistently; no ALL CAPS sentences (one word for emphasis is fine).
- Never promise what the page doesn't deliver.

## Facts: only what the source says

Use only facts from the page or from the user: the count, price, time, audience and benefits they
actually state. Don't add selling points of your own ("no remodel needed", "renter-friendly",
"kid-approved", "ready in 10 minutes") even if they seem likely. If a strong angle needs a fact you
don't have, leave it out or ask the user; never write it in and flag it afterwards.
If you haven't seen the pin image, write alt text from the photo you chose (or describe the planned
image) and say it should match the final image.

## Descriptions

See [references/description-and-seo.md](references/description-and-seo.md) for structure, examples,
alt text and boards. In short: open with the keyword and the benefit, add 1-2 sentences of
specifics, end with a soft CTA. Natural sentences, not keyword lists. Hashtags are optional and add
little; if used, 2-3 at most at the end.

## Output format

When delivering copy, give it ready to paste:

```
Headline (on image): ...
Subtitle (on image): ...
Pin title: ...
Description: ...
Alt text: ...
Board: ...
Keywords used: primary - ...; secondary - ...
```
